# Summarize Program Results

Synthesize quantitative results and staff or participant feedback into recipient-ready copy plus a separate staff-only review note. Start from attached files or pasted tables; an organization context file is optional.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the reporting context, intended destination, supplied goals, and any stated decision or learning use, then create a source ledger using `../../references/source-and-consent.md`. If the destination is missing, add `[NEEDS INPUT: destination]` to the staff-only note. Identify reporting period, population, measure definitions, available collection method, response rate, missingness, and who collected or summarized feedback. Ask for an unavailable detail only when the destination requires it or it materially affects interpretation; a usable source description is not a reason to invent the underlying collection procedure.
2. Recalculate every reported percentage, change, and derived count. Show numerator and denominator, eligible population, exclusions or missing responses, reporting period, and rounding. Before returning the output, compare every number in tables and narrative with its source or visible derivation, including prose counts, target progress, coverage, and missing-response totals. Confirm that subtraction and comparison operands describe compatible populations and units. When displaying a calculation, prefer `16/20 = 80%` with a clear population label, rather than the division symbol. Counts with percentages in prose are also suitable; do not force equations into every sentence. Preserve the disclosure safeguards below. Never report a bare percentage.
3. Organize findings by goal or outcome, beginning with the outcome or decision finding most material to the supplied goal or use. Do not lead with delivery volume by default when another supported finding is more decision-relevant. Retain material contrary, null, or unfavorable findings instead of prioritizing by positive sentiment. For each finding, separate the observed result, any staff- or participant-reported interpretation, and what remains unknown. Carry the source's qualifier into the final prose; do not turn an attributed explanation into an established cause.
4. Assess response coverage: who was invited, who responded, who may be missing, whether repeat respondents or duplicate records are present, and whether feedback collected or summarized by staff differs from direct responses. For small samples or subgroups, show exact counts and missingness only when disclosure is safe for the stated destination; otherwise generalize or omit the identifying value and record the exact counts and reason in the staff-only note. Avoid precision the evidence cannot support.
5. Before making a comparison, confirm that its definitions, populations, methods, and periods are compatible. If a supplied baseline, target, prior period, or benchmark is not comparable, omit the comparison or present it with the specific limitation instead of implying progress or underperformance.
6. Disaggregate only when a supplied grouping is relevant to the stated use, the evidence supports interpretation, and disclosure is safe. Generalize or omit unstable, underpowered, or identifying cuts and record why; do not add demographic breakdowns by default.
7. Use quotation marks only for exact source wording with documented consent for this use and destination. Record consent scope, fidelity, attribution, source location, and collection path in the quote log. If wording changes, remove quotation marks and label it as an attributed paraphrase. If consent is absent or unclear, omit the restricted words and state that no usable quote is available.

## Output

Return concise recipient-ready copy with:

- reporting period and population definition;
- an outcomes table with measure, numerator/denominator, result, a recipient-suitable source description, method, and limitations;
- feedback themes noting who collected the feedback and who may be missing;
- a clearly labeled limitations and missing-data section;
- a quote log (or `No consented quotations available`).

After the recipient-ready copy, add a clearly labeled **Staff-only review note** containing stable source IDs, provenance, staff questions, and: `Staff must verify the sources and interpretation, resolve the flagged questions, and decide whether to revise, approve, hold, or send.` Record `Destination: [stated destination]`; if none was supplied, record `Destination: [NEEDS INPUT: destination]` and say staff must decide the intended use. Do not place the staff-only note, internal source IDs, approval details, or reviewer notes inside recipient-facing copy.

Use participant/client/member/student/patient terminology supplied by the organization. Avoid causal language unless the supplied evaluation design supports causal inference; a before/after pattern, registration-to-attendance comparison, or staff belief does not establish causation. Prefer “was associated with,” “participants reported,” “staff reported,” or “the records show,” as supported.

## Editorial approach

Write for the stated reader and decision. Use plain, direct sentences that explain what happened, what it means, the material limitation, and the next action. Keep the tone professional and measured; avoid promotional language, unnecessary legal phrasing, and technical labels when ordinary words preserve the meaning.

Keep limitations that affect interpretation beside the finding. Consolidate repeated caveats in the limitations section rather than repeating them throughout the brief. Put detailed calculation checks and consequential documentation requests in the staff-only note; do not create an inventory of unavailable methods or optional analyses. Retain the required recipient-facing measures, sources, available methods, denominators and material missingness; brevity must not obscure them.

Use participant voices only when supported and consented; do not invent a story or quotation to make a report warmer. End with specific, evidence-supported learning or next steps when relevant to the requested use.

## What not to do

- Do not fabricate a quote, consent record, denominator, response rate, or outcome.
- Do not treat staff observations as participant self-report.
- Do not present a small or selective response set as representative without qualification.
- Do not merge incompatible periods or definitions to make a trend.
- Do not finish with a claim that the summary is approved; staff verify sources and interpretation and retain the decision to revise, approve, hold, or send.

## Example
