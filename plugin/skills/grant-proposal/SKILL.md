# Draft a Grant Proposal

Draft an application grounded in the funder's supplied instructions and the organization's actual materials. Attach the guidelines or RFP, program description, results, budget, organization context, and a voice sample when available. Past proposals are reference material, not permission to reuse stale facts.

Read `../../references/source-and-consent.md` before drafting.

## Workflow

1. Extract every required question, word limit, attachment, date, budget field, and formatting rule from the funder instructions. Give each distinct requirement its own map row; group only identical fields. If no funder guidelines or RFP were supplied, state `[NEEDS INPUT: funder instructions]`, use a clearly labeled generic outline, and do not imply that it follows the funder's requirements.
2. Build a source map linking each proposed fact, number, named person, program detail, and budget amount to a current applicable source and period. Preserve whether content is observed, reported, proposed, or approved. Do not resolve a conflict by date alone; mark `[CONFLICT — STAFF TO VERIFY]` unless the supplied evidence establishes authority and scope. For each unsupported assertion the application actually needs, create a stable gap record and link the draft marker to it—for example, `[NEEDS SOURCE: GAP-01]`; never register the gap as a source or treat it as evidence.
3. Reconcile the request amount, sum of line items, total program budget, match or cost share, indirect costs, and budget period. Show the calculation and source for each available value. Use supplied negative facts such as `no match required` or `indirect costs not allowed`; do not ask staff to reconfirm them. Flag every real mismatch or missing rule with `[NEEDS INPUT]` or `[CONFLICT — STAFF TO VERIFY]`; never silently fill a gap.
4. Draft in the funder's required order and word limits. For each section, count every limited response by the funder's rule and shorten overages. Use the organization's voice sample where it does not conflict with required headings or instructions.
5. Describe need, approach, outcomes, learning, capacity, evaluation, and budget narrative only to the level supported by the sources. Distinguish historical results from proposed targets and staff-reported context from independently supported facts. A current funder priority or eligibility rule can establish fit, but it does not date the organization's evidence of need. State the supplied period for need evidence; if none is supplied, preserve the source statement and its missing-period gap in the staff appendix, and do not invent a year or imply recency. In recipient copy, retain supported program scope and target-group identity without presenting an undated barrier, prevalence, trend, or urgency statement as current need. If the destination requires empirical need evidence, use a neutral missing-evidence placeholder where permitted and put the specific source request in the staff appendix. When the funder supplies priorities, eligibility rules, or selection criteria, connect each material point to supported facts about the program, population, geography, and request; do not substitute praise or copied funder language for evidence of fit. Administrative or eligibility facts need an `as of` date when currency matters, not an invented program reporting period.
6. Check the proposal as one delivery case: the need and population should lead to the stated approach, outputs, outcomes, measures, request, and budget without changing scope between sections. Test activities, milestones, timing, reach, staffing, costs, and evaluation duties against supplied capacity and funding. Surface any material reliance on partners, continuation funding, assumptions, risks, or contingencies that the funder requests or that is necessary to understand delivery. Do not infer a named partner, species, site detail, approval, or other specificity from a broader supported statement. When approved sources conflict or a newer option is not approved, preserve both positions and leave the authorized choice explicitly unresolved. Keep competing draft options, their dependencies, and the approval choice in the staff-only appendix; recipient copy should use a neutral missing-choice placeholder where the destination permits one, without selecting an option or exposing internal deliberation.
7. End with a staff-only evidence and completeness appendix containing the requirement map, source notes, reconciliation, conflicts, gaps, and staff owner/questions.

Before returning the proposal, run this preflight against the actual recipient copy and appendix:

- Check every funder field separately against its requirement-map row, source or gap, period where relevant, response limit, and recipient/staff placement. Do not infer that a section-level review covered its individual fields.
- Recalculate every displayed budget total from its stated components using the funder's definitions. In particular, distinguish a program-cost subtotal from a total that adds other cash, match, or cost share. Confirm that narrative totals, tables, request amounts, and reconciliation notes agree. Record the supplied indirect-cost rule; if none is supplied, keep one explicit unresolved rule gap alongside the budget assumptions in the staff appendix. Do not assume that indirect costs are allowed or disallowed.
- First remove source-designated staff-only or confidential content from recipient copy; never reclassify a protected source fact because a draft exposes it. Classify each neutral placeholder by its intended audience. A neutral missing value or choice shown in recipient copy is recipient-facing; only its source request, approval dependency, owner, or completion procedure is staff-only. Do not label recipient placeholders `staff_only` merely because staff must resolve them.
- Scan proposed outcomes and design features for causal upgrades. When evidence supports only intent or design, use language such as `is intended to`, `is designed to`, or `may help`; do not change it to `will improve`, `will make`, or another guaranteed effect.
- Classify each population statement before demanding a need-evidence period. Funder-defined eligibility, target-group identity, program scope, and design rationale can be reported as such from their supplied source. An empirical barrier, prevalence, trend, urgency, or current-need claim requires its evidence period or a staff-side source gap; never invent the period.

## Output structure

Return two clearly separated parts:

1. **Recipient-facing application:** clean application copy in the funder's required order and limits. Do not insert source IDs or reviewer notes into clean funder prose. A visible `[NEEDS INPUT: ...]` remains blocking until staff replaces it and rechecks the affected limit. Use neutral `[NEEDS INPUT: <missing value or choice>]` placeholders only where the destination permits them. Explicit task or field restrictions override generic placeholder guidance; if a field permits only a contact or another narrow value, do not insert workflow instructions or unrelated gaps there. Never ask staff to verify, transfer, certify, upload, approve, or submit in recipient copy; put sources, owners, and competing draft options in a matching staff-only gap.
   - Preserve supplied field labels and certification, attestation, consent, and funder-instruction text verbatim. Replace only the missing human response or control with `[NEEDS INPUT: <missing certification response>]`; keep model-authored workflow commentary staff-only.
2. **Staff-only evidence and completeness appendix:** requirement-to-draft map, source notes for quantitative and named claims, budget reconciliation, conflicts, gaps, and staff questions. Source IDs and reviewer notes belong here and do not count toward funder limits.

## Evidence and human review

Show a source note for each quantitative or named claim in the staff-only appendix. If a required number, contact, deadline, budget line, or result is absent, use `[NEEDS INPUT: ...]`; do not infer it from a prior document. If the packet supplies the answer, including a negative rule or zero requirement, use it instead of creating a question. If the packet supports a useful but non-required observation, label it as a reviewer note in the appendix rather than forcing it into the application.

Before staff approval, run `/nonprofit:review-before-sending` on the draft and source packet. That review can flag claims, inconsistent numbers, personal information, and quote consent; it does not replace staff judgment.

## What not to do

- Do not invent an executive director, funder priority, award history, partner, statistic, match, or budget.
- Do not promise a future result merely because it appeared as a past result.
- Do not claim a causal effect from descriptive program data.
- Do not silently copy a quote or named story from a prior application when current consent is not documented.
- Do not use legal, privacy, or regulatory labels as if this workflow certifies compliance.
- Do not present a funder's current priority as proof that the organization's need evidence is current, and do not put competing internal options or approval instructions into recipient copy.

## Example

For the fictional packet, track requirements as `R1: 300-word need statement`, `R2: $15,000 request`, and `R3: two outcome measures`. S2 supports the `$15,000` request requirement, while S1 supplies only a `$12,000` program budget: flag the `$3,000` gap as `[CONFLICT — STAFF TO VERIFY]` and do not invent line items, match, or cost share. Also mark the allowable indirect-cost rule `[NEEDS INPUT]` because S2 says it is absent. Requirement IDs are not source IDs.
